\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On \emph{synthetic} sine+cliff tiles, DenoiseOpt improves locked $R_{\mathrm{blend}}$ with compact operators searched by hybrid GA+PPO(+PBT) and latency-aware $J$; natural factory and AKWF seams remain a negative result (Section~\ref{sec:real-natural-seam}).
I reuse existing search tools; I do not introduce a new NAS algorithm.
Noise2Noise is the neural gate. Dual Cosine is the classical fade.

On five holdout seeds, short-FitCell hybrid scores $0.971{\pm}0.001$: below Noise2Noise ($0.977{\pm}0.002$), far above Dual Cosine ($0.542{\pm}0.038$); a ten-seed holdout confirms this gap ($0.9710{\pm}0.0029$ vs $0.9764{\pm}0.0020$, paired $\Delta{=}{-}0.0053$, $p{=}0.0020$).
Multi-family board means show no significant directional difference from Noise2Noise under short FitCell (sign-test $p{=}1.0$; not equivalence).
Hard cliffs collapse classical fades while searched operators hold.
A matched $5$k bake-off gives hybrid the top descriptive mean ($0.9748{\pm}0.0018$; $n{=}3$ search seeds; vs runner-up $\Delta{=}{+}0.0114$, $p{=}0.25$), so compute goes to hybrid-only FitCell-to-plateau without treating the ranking as formal superiority.
FitCell-to-plateau across three search seeds reaches $0.9881{\pm}0.0029$ on holdout batches, descriptively above the ten-seed N2N mean $0.9764$ on $3/3$ search draws (exact sign-flip $p{=}0.25$ at $n{=}3$; Section~\ref{sec:v14-converge}).
Cross-domain transfer boards are wrap-protocol stress tests (Appendix~\ref{app:transfer-bundle}).
No formal listening study was run; the claim is objective residual.
